I published an op-ed in **[EcoMatin](https://ecomatin.net/)**, a Cameroonian business daily, on 21 August 2026 in its Opinions section, under the title “À Douala, l'électricité ne manque pas : elle n'arrive pas assez” (“In Douala, electricity is not what is missing — the delivery is”). I open it with a tribute to Alain Malong, former managing director of Alucam and former president of Syndustricam, who died in early August and who spent forty years arguing for power that is reliable, not merely available.

My argument: Cameroon's economic capital suffers less from a shortage of generation than from a weak grid. The Southern Interconnected Grid has more capacity than national demand, yet according to the regulator, more than two-thirds of the energy not supplied over the past five years is down to transmission and distribution. The Edéa–Douala link is saturated and, when load has to be shed, industry is cut off before households, in a city that accounts for 31.2% of national GDP.

I put forward three avenues: a common rule for large consumers' requests to connect directly to the transmission grid, with a tariff that makes them share fixed costs, instead of case-by-case decisions; a share of the decisions now taken in Yaoundé handed back to the local level; and industrial self-generation that moves from diesel to rooftop solar and to cogeneration using Logbaba gas. Turning ENEO into SOCADEL addresses the sector's financial health; it adds no capacity to the lines.
