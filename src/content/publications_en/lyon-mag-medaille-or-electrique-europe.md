I have a contribution in **[Lyon Mag](https://www.lyonmag.com/article/154070/le-jour-ou-notre-region-est-devenue-la-medaille-d-or-electrique-de-l-europe)**, published on 21 September 2026 under the title “Le jour où notre région est devenue la médaille d'or électrique de l'Europe”.

In it I establish that since 2023 Auvergne-Rhône-Alpes has been the European region that generates the most electricity, a change of rank that neither the Region nor the operators made known. North Rhine-Westphalia, long in first place, fell back after Germany's nuclear exit and the faster phase-out of lignite, while the French region generated 119 terawatt-hours last year. I explain this lead through history, drawing on the work of Alain Beltran: Alpine hydropower and the development of the Rhône came before the atom, and nuclear plants were later built along the same river. The result is a fleet that pairs steady output with dams able to follow demand, almost carbon-free.

I set out two limits myself: with no official European ranking, I reason by elimination, and German figures leave out the electricity that industry generates for its own needs, which narrows the gap without closing it. Finally, I note that the Region does not claim this rank in the paper it submitted to the public debate on the grid, and that the Rhône, on which this output rests, remains vulnerable to climate change.
