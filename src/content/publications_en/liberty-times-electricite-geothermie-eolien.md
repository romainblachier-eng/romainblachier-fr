I have an op-ed in the **[自由時報 (Liberty Times)](https://talk.ltn.com.tw/article/breakingnews/5479391)** — one of Taiwan's largest daily newspapers — on how the island should meet its rising demand for electricity.

## What is the 自由時報 (Liberty Times)?

The **自由時報** (*Liberty Times*) is one of Taiwan's three major dailies and among the most widely read on the island. Publishing there means speaking directly into Taiwan's public debate, in its own language.

## The gist of the op-ed

Taiwan's electricity demand is surging, driven in part by the rise of artificial intelligence. The government's answer — adding four gas-fired power plants — misses the core issue: Taiwan already imports **97% of its energy**, and its liquefied natural gas reserves cover only **eleven to fourteen days**. Betting further on gas deepens a vulnerability rather than reducing it.

Yet the island has resources beneath its own feet and off its own shores:

- **Geothermal energy**: an estimated potential of **10 to 32 GW**, while only **7 MW** are exploited today.
- **Offshore wind**: the **Hai Long** wind farm will reach **1,022 MW** in 2026, producing roughly **4,500 GWh** per year.

My argument is straightforward: Taiwan's energy security depends on power generated locally — underground and at sea — rather than on fuels it will always have to import.
