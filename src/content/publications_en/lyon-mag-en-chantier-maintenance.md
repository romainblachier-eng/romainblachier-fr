Op-ed published in **[Lyon Mag](https://www.lyonmag.com/article/153382/en-chantier-nous-ralons-contre-ce-qui-empeche-la-ville-de-tomber-en-panne)** on 3 August 2026, starting from the roadworks that saturate the Lyon urban area every summer — and what they reveal about an urban machinery we only see when it fails.

## Star and Ruhleder: invisible infrastructure

**Susan Leigh Star and Karen Ruhleder** (*Steps Toward an Ecology of Infrastructure*, 1996) established that as long as an infrastructure works, it blends into everyday life. Nobody thinks about the water network when opening a tap; you start thinking about it when nothing comes out. The summer works — connections to the heating and cooling networks around Part-Dieu, renewal of the heating network on rue Baraban, a sewer collector in Caluire-et-Cuire, slope stabilisation on boulevard de la Duchère, connecting the T9 in Villeurbanne, the T10 between Saint-Fons and Vénissieux — are the moment this invisible layer surfaces.

## Forty project owners on a single street

Up to forty project owners can work simultaneously on Lyon's public space, each with its own schedules and constraints. What residents perceive as disorder is first of all a coordination problem — and the irritation is aimed less at the works themselves than at the absence of any explanation of who does what, when and why.

## Huré: technology as lever or constraint

**Maxime Huré** ("Expertise, infrastructures, réseaux. La technique, levier ou entrave à l'action publique urbaine?", 2021) points to the ambivalence: technology gives local governments material means to act, but narrows their room for manoeuvre when they depend on a multitude of operators and timetables they do not control.

## The inauguration/maintenance trade-off

The summer controversy over Lyon's fountains illustrates the asymmetry: maintenance is invisible as long as it is done. If upkeep is carried out, the breakdown never happens and nobody knows it was avoided; if it is postponed, the budget saving is immediate and the bill arrives later, often on someone else's watch. Hence the conclusion: you can grumble at a badly organised worksite — what should really worry you is when nothing is being repaired.
