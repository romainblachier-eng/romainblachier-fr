Op-ed published in **[Lyon Mag](https://www.lyonmag.com/article/153382/en-chantier-nous-ralons-contre-ce-qui-empeche-la-ville-de-tomber-en-panne)** on 3 August 2026 under the title “En chantier : nous râlons contre ce qui empêche la ville de tomber en panne”. I start from the roadworks that clutter the Lyon urban area in summer, from the heating networks around Part-Dieu to the T10 tram line, to argue that these disruptions are often the visible side of the maintenance the city depends on.

I draw on Susan Leigh Star and Karen Ruhleder, for whom infrastructure only gets noticed when it stops serving, and on Maxime Huré, for whom technical tools enable local governments to act while making them dependent on operators and timetables beyond their control. With up to forty project owners liable to work in the street at the same time, the disorder people feel is first of all a coordination failure.

Finally, the piece points to a political bias, illustrated by the summer row over Lyon's fountains: an avoided breakdown is never seen, whereas putting off maintenance yields an immediate saving and leaves the bill to a later term of office. My conclusion: a badly run worksite deserves criticism, but the absence of repairs should worry us far more.
