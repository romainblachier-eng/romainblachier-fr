I have an analysis in the **[Taipei Times](https://www.taipeitimes.com/News/feat/archives/2026/10/13/2003865856)** titled “Taiwan’s nuclear restart faces an environmental review dispute”. It appeared on page 12, in the Features section, on 13 October 2026. Its standfirst sums up the issue: regulators have approved Ma-anshan’s initial restart plan, the environment ministry says no environmental impact assessment is needed, but critics argue the law requires one.

## Where the file stands

On 24 September, the Nuclear Safety Commission (NSC, 核安會) approved the re-operation plan Taipower had filed in March for the **Ma-anshan** plant in Pingtung County, after **three rounds of review and 589 comments**. Taipower now has to carry out the work in the plan and its own inspections on ageing, radiation and seismic resistance, then report back to the commission for review and on-site checks. Minister of Economic Affairs Kung Ming-hsin (龔明鑫) has named **2028 as the earliest restart**. The NSC has given no date.

## What the law says

The disagreement turns on **Article 29** of the implementing criteria of the Environmental Impact Assessment Act. It requires an assessment to build a nuclear plant, add units or decommission one, and **does not mention licence renewal**. The ministry last amended the criteria on 5 February, and the nuclear item still lists those three cases.

Positions have shifted. In May 2025, Minister of Environment Peng Chi-ming (彭啟明) asked: “Why does a restart or a life extension need no EIA, when decommissioning needs one?” He said the act would undergo review. But on 22 March he said current law requires no assessment for a life extension, and that the NSC’s substantive review already resembles one. In December 2025, NSC Chairperson Chen Ming-chen (陳明真) said the commission had already assessed the environmental effects of radiation and would “take full responsibility.”

## A question of timing

No judge has yet ruled. If a suit is filed once the licence has been issued, that is when the procedure would be judged, that is, after the inspections are over. I set the **Belgian case** beside it: after the extension of reactors such as Doel 1 and 2, the question of environmental assessment went to court, all the way to the Court of Justice of the European Union.

## What I propose

Settle the question **before** the licence, within the inspection years that remain. That would fix the legal basis of the licence before anyone asks a judge, and give the groups opposed to the restart a procedure to take part in rather than a lawsuit to wait for.
