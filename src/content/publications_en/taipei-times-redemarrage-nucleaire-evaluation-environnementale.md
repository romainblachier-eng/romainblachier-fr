I have an analysis in the **[Taipei Times](https://www.taipeitimes.com/News/feat/archives/2026/10/13/2003865856)**, published on 13 October 2026 on page 12 of the Features section: “Taiwan’s nuclear restart faces an environmental review dispute”.

On 24 September, the Nuclear Safety Commission (核安會) approved the plan Taipower had submitted to bring the Ma-anshan plant back into service; works and inspections remain to be done, and the economic affairs minister does not see a restart before 2028. The analysis deals with a question still left open: must the plant go through an environmental impact assessment before it starts up again? The environment ministry says no, associations and legal scholars argue the opposite, and the criteria implementing the law are silent on renewing a licence. A Belgian precedent: in 2019, the Court of Justice of the European Union required an assessment to extend Doel 1 and 2, and the litigation continued once the extension was granted.

My proposal: settle the matter before a judge does. The ministry can clarify Article 29 of those criteria; the commission can use its own review rules to request environmental reports. The years of inspection leave time to act, and opponents of the restart would gain a procedure in which to be heard.
