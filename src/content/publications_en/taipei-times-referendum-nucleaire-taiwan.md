I have an analysis in the **[Taipei Times](https://www.taipeitimes.com/News/feat/archives/2026/11/21/2003868117)**, published on 21 November 2026 on page 12 of the Features section: “What Taiwan’s nuclear referendum can change”.

On 28 November, alongside the local elections, voters will answer a question the KMT wrote and passed through the legislature with TPP support: should Taiwan drop its “nuclear-free homeland” policy? The piece asks what a yes would mean in law. Since lawmakers called the vote, it can only settle a matter of policy, not strike down a statute: the government would have to act and its agencies could not reverse course for two years, yet two laws, on the environment and on climate change, would still set the goal of a Taiwan without nuclear power. Changing them is up to the legislature.

The article also points out that the official referendum gazette carries no view from the ministries on its legal effects, a step the law waives when the legislature initiates a vote. It draws on an interview with Su Yen-tu, who studies law at Academia Sinica and doubts that the question is a genuine policy choice.
