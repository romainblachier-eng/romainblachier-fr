I published an op-ed in **[Lyon Mag](https://www.lyonmag.com/article/153698/quand-le-rhone-devient-trop-chaud-pour-le-nucleaire)** on 24 August 2026, titled “Quand le Rhône devient trop chaud pour le nucléaire”.

The piece starts from the shutdown of reactor 3 at Bugey on 11 August, as the Rhône neared its temperature threshold; the next day, thirteen of France's fifty-seven reactors were stopped or running at reduced power for environmental reasons. My argument is about timing rather than volume: the output lost is small, but it goes missing during heatwaves, when air conditioning pushes consumption up and neighbouring countries are under strain too, a risk I had described in [an op-ed for *Le Monde*](https://www.lemonde.fr/idees/article/2026/08/13/centrales-nucleaires-a-l-arret-le-scenario-combinant-canicule-durable-eau-trop-chaude-et-baisse-des-debits-met-a-mal-le-parc-francais_6745802_3232.html). In the longer run, retreating Alpine glaciers are altering the river's summer flow and sharpening competition between its uses.

My main focus is how responsibility is shared. The City, the Metropolis and the Region decide neither the temperature of the Rhône nor the availability of reactors, which fall to the safety authority, EDF, RTE and the State; yet they do have levers, from building retrofits to climate plans, and that is what they should be judged on. I conclude that warming, driven above all by fossil fuels, weakens both nuclear and hydropower: all the more reason to leave fossil fuels behind.
