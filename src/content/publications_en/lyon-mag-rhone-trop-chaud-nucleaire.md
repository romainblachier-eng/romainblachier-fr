I published an op-ed in **[Lyon Mag](https://www.lyonmag.com/article/153698/quand-le-rhone-devient-trop-chaud-pour-le-nucleaire)** on what the warming of the Rhône does to the region's power generation.

## The argument

It starts from a paradox: climate change, driven first by fossil fuels, undermines two of the least carbon-intensive sources of electricity we have — nuclear and hydropower — because both depend on water.

- **Once-through cooling.** At Bugey, two of the four reactors draw water from the Rhône to cool themselves, then return it to the river warmer than they took it.
- **What the heat does to the river.** During a heat spike, water that is too warm degrades living conditions for fish and disturbs the ecosystem. That environmental limit is what constrains output — not a technical failure.
- **The shutdown is a choice, not an accident.** The operator stopped the reactor before the temperature limit was crossed. The system worked as designed; the problem is how often the situation now arises.
- **What follows.** Future flows and temperatures belong in the design of new reactors and in the planning of the power system, rather than being treated as a summer contingency.
