Op-ed published in **[Lyon Mag](https://www.lyonmag.com/article/153266/le-climatiseur-et-le-platane)** on 28 July 2026 under the title “Le climatiseur et le platane”. In it I examine three decisions taken in the Lyon urban area during the heatwave, among them the abandonment of the Rive Droite project, which was to turn the Rhône riverbanks into a tree-lined promenade, and the purchase of mobile air conditioners by the Region: three responses to the same heat that do not fit together.

I read them with the help of political science. Michael Bauer and his colleagues classify the ways a public policy can be undone; Alan Jacobs explains why a project with immediate costs and distant benefits is exposed to being dropped; Sarah Kehler and her co-authors observe that in local climate adaptation, the level in charge of planning rarely has the means to act, which pushes towards visible, reversible gestures; Nora Förell's team describes how vague vocabulary ends up preventing discussion.

My conclusion is institutional. A study published in *Climate Policy*, comparing Denmark with the United Kingdom, sets out two ways of making long-term commitments credible; French metropolitan areas, Lyon included, apply neither, so a new majority is not bound by the choices of the previous one. The promised plane tree would only have given shade around 2045.
