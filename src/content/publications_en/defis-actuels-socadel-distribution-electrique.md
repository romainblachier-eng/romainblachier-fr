I published an op-ed in **[Défis Actuels](https://www.newsducamer.com/socadel-defi-distribution-electrique-cameroun/)** (Cameroon), issue no. 1142, Tuesday 18 August 2026, *Horizons* section, page 11, under the title “Socadel héritera des compteurs, pas de la capacité : le vrai chantier de la distribution électrique camerounaise” (“Socadel will inherit the meters, not the capacity: the real challenge for Cameroon's power distribution”). **Défis Actuels** is a Cameroonian daily covering economic affairs, billed on its own masthead as the country's "premier news magazine".

The piece deals with the takeover of electricity distribution by Socadel, the new public operator. I argue that what matters is less the equipment handed over than the new operator's ability to run the network, and that everything is decided in the first eighteen months. Three tasks strike me as neglected: the quality of the data received, which warrants an independent audit before the handover ends; inherited contracts, to be ranked by how vital they are to the service; and staff holding unwritten know-how, whose retention must be budgeted for.

That leaves large industrial users' requests to connect directly to the transmission grid. Rather than being for or against, I propose rules that make these departures sustainable for the distributor: a power threshold that applies to everyone, a transmission tariff that keeps them contributing to public service, an exit timetable and a regulator equipped to arbitrate.
