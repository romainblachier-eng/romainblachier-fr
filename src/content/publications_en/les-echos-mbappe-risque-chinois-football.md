I published an op-ed (in French) in **[Les Echos](https://www.lesechos.fr/idees-debats/cercle/affaire-mbappe-les-ligues-et-les-clubs-devraient-appliquer-aux-recettes-chinoises-une-decote-pour-risque-politique-comme-le-fait-un-investisseur-dans-un-pays-instable-2260741)**, in its Le Cercle section, on 3 November 2026: "Affaire Mbappé : « Les ligues et les clubs devraient appliquer aux recettes chinoises une décote pour risque politique, comme le fait un investisseur dans un pays instable »".

On 27 October, Kylian Mbappé lost his three Chinese sponsors for laughing at a montage that showed him as Mao; Chinese state television dropped all of La Liga, and apologies from La Liga and then Real Madrid achieved nothing. Seen through an investor's eyes, the affair shows that the state broadcaster, brands and Chinese social networks reacted together to the same political signal, so this revenue forms a single risk, not a diversified portfolio, as the NBA learned back in 2019.

Rather than piling up apologies, football can handle this risk through contracts, with three levers: for player unions, morality clauses that leave out opinions unrelated to the brand, or a termination payment; for leagues, broadcasters bound to keep paying even when a suspension has nothing to do with sport; for clubs, a discount on revenue coming from China.
