I published an op-ed in **[Le Temps](https://www.letemps.ch/opinions/taiwan-ou-le-retour-de-la-souverainete-electrique)**, Switzerland's leading daily, on 28 July 2026: “Taïwan ou le retour de la souveraineté électrique” (“Taiwan, or the return of electric sovereignty”).

I start from a paradox: the island that makes most of the world's leading-edge semiconductors buys almost all its energy abroad and has no power interconnection with the outside world, while TSMC could account for nearly a quarter of Taiwan's consumption around 2030, according to S&P Global. I then look at the possible connections. Plugging Kinmen and Matsu into the Chinese grid, as Beijing has proposed since 2019, would hand it leverage; Taiwan is looking instead at a subsea link to the Philippines.

The op-ed goes on to compare Taiwan with Ireland, which now requires data centres to bring their own dispatchable capacity, and with Cameroon, rich in electricity but short of grid: two ways of running into the gap between the power to decide and the capacity to act. It ends with France, whose abundant, low-carbon electricity remains underused as long as its transport depends on oil.
