## The argument: an exit that is not a rupture

On 28 April 2026, the United Arab Emirates announced their withdrawal from OPEC and OPEC+. The immediate reading saw it as a consequence of the Hormuz crisis. I argue for another one: the withdrawal is the **culmination of a trajectory** that began long before, whose first public signal was Abu Dhabi's challenge to the cartel's quotas in 2021.

The argument rests on the notion of a **rent window**. The Emirates arbitrate in favour of extracting maximum value from their oil rent while it can still be monetised, which requires shedding the collective discipline that caps production. That trade-off also explains the gap between the Emirati narrative on the transition — the COP28 presidency, investment in renewables — and the actual structure of the country's investment.

The consequence for Europeans: Gulf producers are partners **during** the transition rather than partners **in** the transition, and cooperation is better built on the segments where interests genuinely overlap.
