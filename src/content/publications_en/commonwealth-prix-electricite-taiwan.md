I have an op-ed in **[CommonWealth Magazine](https://english.cw.com.tw/article/article.action?id=4772)** (天下雜誌), in its *Insight / Op-eds* section, published on 15 May 2026: "Why Electricity Prices That Are Too Low Could Hurt Taiwan".

**CommonWealth Magazine** is a major Taiwanese business magazine, founded in 1981 by journalist Diane Ying (殷允芃). In 2007 it became the island's first business outlet to launch an English edition.

Taiwan's energy debate revolves around nuclear power and renewables; the op-ed looks at what it overlooks, the tariff. Taiwan's electricity is among the cheapest in the world, households pay well below what it costs to generate, and the tariff review committee nonetheless froze prices in March 2026. This low price is a political choice that nothing pays for: the shortfall piles up at Taipower, whose accumulated losses reached NT$417.9 billion at the end of July 2025, and the bill grows with every year of delay.

The freeze also holds back the transition. Up against a regulated tariff that ignores the true cost of fossil generation, offshore wind, solar and geothermal struggle to find buyers for power purchase agreements, while AI will push demand up and the grid needs heavy investment.
