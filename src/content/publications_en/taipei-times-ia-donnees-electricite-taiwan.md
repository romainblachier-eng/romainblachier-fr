I wrote a feature for the **[Taipei Times](https://www.taipeitimes.com/News/feat/archives/2026/08/29/2003863315)**, published on 29 August 2026: **“Taiwan's AI strategy has a public data problem”**.

Taiwan is building new gas-fired plants to keep pace with the rise of artificial intelligence, yet the public figures on data-center demand do not match. The Energy Administration's estimates, the connection requests received by Taipower and the data published by the outlet *The Reporter* add up to different totals, and no note explains how to reconcile them; only a small part of these volumes corresponds to facilities actually hooked up to the grid. The stakes are high for a country without any cross-border gas pipeline, which receives all its gas by sea and whose Ma-anshan nuclear plant has been offline since May 2025.

My proposal: Taipower should release an anonymized queue of grid-connection requests, broken down by municipality and substation, that sets preliminary requests apart from firm commitments, and the Energy Administration should update its range for AI-related demand and spell out its assumptions. Relying more on imported gas would remain a political choice, but lawmakers, municipalities and the residents concerned could check the calculations behind it.
