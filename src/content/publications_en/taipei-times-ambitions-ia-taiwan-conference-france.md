I published an op-ed in the **[Taipei Times](https://www.taipeitimes.com/News/editorials/archives/2026/09/18/2003864452)**, in the Editorial & Opinion section, on 18 September 2026: “Realizing Taiwan's AI ambitions”.

It follows on from the Taiwan-France Economic Cooperation Conference, organised in Paris by MEDEF International and the Chinese International Economic Cooperation Association, in which I took part. My argument: in Taiwan, the future of artificial intelligence is now being decided on the power grid. A large share of the data-center connection requests that Taipower has accepted lapse before any formal filing, which makes it hard to size substations and lines; France faces the same difficulty and handles it by giving precedence to the most advanced projects. I also argue that no single technology settles everything: gas, nuclear, offshore wind and geothermal each expose the island to a different kind of dependence.

The op-ed asks Taiwan and France to go beyond statements of principle: set their AI- and chip-driven demand forecasts side by side, swap know-how on network planning, and pick a handful of projects whose results can be checked. Cooperation would run both ways, since Taiwanese manufacturers such as Fortune Electric are able to supply equipment to European grids as well.
