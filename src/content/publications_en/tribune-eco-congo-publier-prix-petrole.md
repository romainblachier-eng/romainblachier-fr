I published an op-ed in **[Tribune Eco](https://www.tribune-eco.cg/romain-blachier-publier-les-prix-du-petrole-pour-mieux-vendre-le-brut-congolais/)** on 17 October 2026: “Publier les prix du pétrole pour mieux vendre le brut congolais” (“Publishing oil prices to sell Congolese crude better”).

This summer, crude from the Republic of the Congo sold on average at a discount of more than six dollars to Brent. The state, paid mainly in kind, loses on two fronts: the price set by the pricing committee decides the share of barrels it receives once the companies' costs are recovered, and the price obtained by the Société nationale des pétroles du Congo (SNPC) decides what it earns from them. With oil providing about 40% of public revenue, narrowing this gap would be quicker than raising output: at current production, each dollar recovered is worth 3,000 extra barrels a day.

I propose that the Ministry of Hydrocarbons publish, each quarter alongside the set prices, a public page showing, crude by crude, how many cargoes were sold, at what prices, and how each compares with crudes of similar quality, using the Bacon and Tordo method (World Bank, 2004), so as to separate the effect of quality from that of sales terms. The state already has the authority to set prices; these data would give it the means to check the sales the companies declare. I followed the same reasoning for Socadel in Cameroon.
