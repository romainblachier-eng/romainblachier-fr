I published an op-ed in **[EcoMatin](https://ecomatin.net/opinions/le-cameroun-est-il-encore-un-pays-de-petroliers)** on 11 November 2026, in its Opinions section, under the title “Le Cameroun est-il encore un pays de pétroliers ?” (“Is Cameroon still a country of oil professionals?”). In it I put to the test a label attached to the country out of habit.

The figures qualify it. Output has collapsed since its 1985 peak, and oil and gas bring in barely more than 9% of the domestic revenue expected in 2026. With its refinery, Sonara, idle since the 2019 fire, the country spent more on imported fuel in 2025 than it earned from its crude; an expensive barrel brings the state revenue, but also raises the cost of holding down pump prices. The real shortfall, in my view, is electricity: installed capacity is still far from the 3,500-megawatt target.

Hence two proposals: make the non-oil primary balance, which the Ministry of Finance already calculates, the pivot of the debate on the 2027 budget; and have SNH, Perenco and their subcontractors second managers for a few years to SOCADEL and Sonatrel. Less of an oil country than people say, Cameroon still has oil professionals whom its power sector needs.
