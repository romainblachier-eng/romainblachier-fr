I published a note for **[Fondation Jean-Jaurès](https://www.jean-jaures.org/publication/taiwan-quand-le-principal-parti-dopposition-bascule-vers-pekin/)** on 29 April 2026: “Taïwan : quand le principal parti d'opposition bascule vers Pékin” (“Taiwan: when the main opposition party pivots towards Beijing”).

**Fondation Jean-Jaurès** is a French think tank, recognised as a public-utility foundation, founded in 1992 and associated with the social-democratic family. Its work is organised around three pillars: studies and research, international cooperation, and history and memory. It regularly publishes notes, investigations and op-eds to inform public debate on the major contemporary transformations — democracy, climate, geopolitics, social cohesion.

The note starts from the trip to China by Cheng Li-wun, head of the Kuomintang, the island's main opposition party, at Xi Jinping's invitation in April 2026: no visit at that level had taken place in ten years. I show that she adopted the language of the Chinese Communist Party and dropped the precautions that, for two decades, had protected Taiwan's sovereignty in exchanges across the Strait.

I examine the consequences: a Taiwanese opposition being reshaped, messages sent to Beijing as well as Washington ahead of local elections, and a comparison with Europe, where some parties also borrow the vocabulary of authoritarian powers.
