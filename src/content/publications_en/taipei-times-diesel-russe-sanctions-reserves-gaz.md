I wrote an op-ed for the **[Taipei Times](https://www.taipeitimes.com/News/editorials/archives/2026/10/21/2003866326)**, published on 21 October 2026 in the Opinion pages: “Beijing was watching diesel deal”.

On 9 October, Washington lifted the penalties on Russian diesel that a law signed by Donald Trump had tightened just three weeks earlier, with diesel above US$6 a gallon ahead of the midterm elections. I draw a lesson for Taiwan: if Washington gives up easy sanctions as soon as they weigh on its own consumers, Beijing has little reason to believe in the ones Taiwan's partners hold up to deter it from a blockade. The piece follows a low-income family whose rice cooker depends on a grid run half on LNG, a gas the island stores for only ten days or so.

I propose treating the 14-day legal minimum for LNG as a floor, paying for a strategic gas reserve out of the state budget and speeding up Taipower's grid resilience plan. Taipei would also do well to build ties with gas storage operators and grid companies in Europe, since Europe has sharply reduced its reliance on Russian gas. In August, I explained for the [CEIAS](/en/publications/ceias/ceias-europe-chip-de-risking-energie/) that European chips rely on Taiwan's power grid.
