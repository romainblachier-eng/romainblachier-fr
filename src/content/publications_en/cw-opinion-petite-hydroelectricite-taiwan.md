I have an op-ed in **[獨立評論@天下](https://opinion.cw.com.tw/blog/profile/52/article/17788)**, the Chinese-language opinion platform of the CommonWealth group, published on 22 September 2026: **《讓送水的管線也發電！台灣小水力與我們的生活》** ("Let the water pipes generate power too! Taiwan's small hydro and our daily life").

The piece is about small hydropower, preferably on structures that already exist: drinking-water mains, channels, weirs. Two installations serve as examples. In Taichung, the Shalu water distribution centre runs a turbine on the 76-metre drop that drinking water descends on its way; in Yilan, the 安農萬富 micro plant generates in a river, with a fish pass.

My position is cautious: no site should be equipped before showing that drinking water, irrigation and river life will not suffer. I propose publishing a readable file for each candidate site (monthly flows, water to be kept as a priority, maintenance outages, grid connection terms), grouping tenders, surveys and maintenance across neighbouring sites, and settling at the outset how maintenance, local benefits and end-of-life dismantling will be handled. It is a supplement: the point is to make use, where the cost allows, of energy that the water dissipates anyway.
