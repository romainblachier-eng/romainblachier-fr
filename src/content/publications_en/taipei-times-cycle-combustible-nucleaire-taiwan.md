My feature in Taiwan's leading English-language daily, the **[Taipei Times](https://www.taipeitimes.com)**, was published on 4 August 2026: *“Taiwan's nuclear restart: what the domestic debate does not decide”*.

Taiwan is debating a return to nuclear power in every venue available to it: the Ma-anshan restart plan, the Nuclear Safety Commission's review, an environmental assessment, and the opposition's referendum proposals for the 28 November local elections. The feature shows that this debate covers only half the problem. The island has no uranium and buys each stage of the fuel cycle abroad, under arrangements it never signed in its own name, with no supply agency of its own and no seat at the IAEA. That market is fragmenting, and Russia and China both hold strong positions in it. As for the fuel bought for Lungmen, a plant that never entered service, it remains stored in the US at Taipower's expense.

My conclusion: Taiwanese can choose to restart their reactors, but their fuel supply, their upkeep and what happens to their waste will hinge on decisions made outside the ballot box: by foreign suppliers, in Washington, and even in a New Taipei City office that has not issued an expected certificate.
