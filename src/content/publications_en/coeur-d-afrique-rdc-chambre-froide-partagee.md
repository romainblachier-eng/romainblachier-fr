I published an op-ed in **[Cœur d'Afrique](https://coeurdafrique.net/article/une-chambre-froide-peut-changer-l-economie-d-un-territoire)**, a news outlet based in Kinshasa, on 19 October 2026 in its Territories section: “Une chambre froide peut changer l'économie d'un territoire” (“A cold room can change a territory's economy”).

I start from Kinsuka-Pêcheur, a neighbourhood in the commune of Ngaliema, where Radio Okapi reported in September 2026 that power was coming on only two days a week. Through an imaginary vendor of drinks and frozen fish, I show what these outages cost: unsold stock marked down at noon and thrown away at night. A university survey of 385 Kinshasa households also links poor power quality to illegal connections.

My proposal: a market cold room, run by a cooperative of women and men vendors who rent space in it by the day, and kept running by solar panels during outages. For SNEL, it would be a single customer, simple to connect. There are precedents in Nigeria, with ColdHubs, and in Goma, with a solar mini-grid. Congolese law allows it: the Constitution leaves the exploitation of non-nuclear energy to the provinces, Kinshasa among them, as I noted in my [paper for the Fondation Jean-Jaurès](/en/publications/jean-jaures/jean-jaures-electricite-afrique-centrale/). Finally, the idea fits the country programming framework signed on 7 October 2026 by the DRC and the FAO, which aims to make the country less dependent on food imports.
