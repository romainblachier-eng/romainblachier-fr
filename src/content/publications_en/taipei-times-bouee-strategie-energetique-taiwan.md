My op-ed in Taiwan's leading English-language daily, the **[Taipei Times](https://www.taipeitimes.com)**, was published on 25 June 2026: *“An energy lifeline is not a strategy”*.

It deals with a bipartisan bill, the *Taiwan Energy Security and Anti-Embargo Act*, which got through the Foreign Relations Committee of the US Senate in February. Its aim is commendable: to secure Taiwan's energy supply if China puts pressure on the island. But it treats the issue almost entirely as a matter of deliveries by sea. That is exactly where Taiwan is weakest: it imports nearly all of its gas, which accounts for a large share of its power generation, and its reserves would only hold out for a few days under a blockade.

My argument: help that leaves Taiwan hanging on tankers, and on a single ally whose disposition may shift, does not add up to a strategy. What truly protects the island is the electricity it generates for itself, out of a blockade's reach, and that is where the effort should go.
