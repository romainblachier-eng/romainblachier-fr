I have just published an article in **Telos**, the online journal founded by Zaki Laïdi, on the United Arab Emirates' exit from OPEC and what it reveals about Emirati energy strategy.

## Telos

**Telos** is an online journal of analysis and debate founded by political scientist Zaki Laïdi. It publishes contributions from academics, researchers and experts on major contemporary economic, geopolitical and social issues. It is a rigorous forum for reflection, situated at the intersection of the academic world and public debate.

## The subject: the UAE's exit from OPEC, the culmination of a fifteen-year trajectory

On 28 April 2026, the UAE announced its withdrawal from both OPEC and OPEC+. The press interpreted this as a rupture triggered by the Iran-Hormuz war. In this article, I argue that **this reading is misleading**: the exit is the culmination of a trajectory set in motion since 2021, when Abu Dhabi publicly challenged the cartel's quotas for the first time.

The Emirati strategy rests on a clear trade-off: **maximising present oil revenue before it runs out**, by freeing itself from the collective disciplines that constrain production. ADNOC has invested $150 billion to bring its capacity to 5 million barrels per day. The timing of the exit — in the midst of the closure of Hormuz — is not opportunistic: it is the least disruptive moment for other producers.

The article also interrogates the Emirati narrative on the energy transition. Despite the presidency of COP28 and investments in Masdar, ADNOC's fossil/renewables capex ratio remains 7 to 1 — well above that of Western majors. Gulf producers are **partners during the transition**, not partners of the transition. Cooperation must be built on segments of genuinely shared interest: civil nuclear, maritime security, critical minerals, and low-carbon hydrogen.
