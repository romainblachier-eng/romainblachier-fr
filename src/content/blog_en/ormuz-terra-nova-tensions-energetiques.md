I just published an op-ed on **La Grande Conversation**, the digital media platform of **Terra Nova**, about what the Strait of Hormuz reveals about our energy dependencies — in Asia as in Europe.

## The subject: Hormuz, revealing energy tensions

The strikes of February 28, 2026 on Iran and the closure of the Strait of Hormuz opened the most serious contemporary oil crisis: Brent above $120, scenarios at $200, 20% of global supply disrupted. In this op-ed, I defend a simple idea: **this crisis is not an isolated episode, it is a revealer**.

A revealer, first, of Asian fragility. 84% of the crude oil that transits through Hormuz goes to Asia. Japan depends on Middle Eastern oil for 93% and has seen its strategic reserves melt to 45 days. South Korea has capped fuel prices at the pump as an emergency measure. India imports 89% of its oil. Taiwan, an isolated island, imports 97% of its primary energy, with just a handful of days of LNG in reserve. Despite massive investments in renewables, the region remains structurally exposed throughout the entire transition period.

A revealer, second, of a French and European paradox. France massively exports the decarbonized electricity it produces, while continuing to import oil and gas for its final uses — mobility, heating, industry. The energy transition is not just a climate policy: **it is now a national security policy**. As long as the electrification of uses does not follow, a shock on Hormuz is paid for in imported inflation, lost competitiveness, and reduced diplomatic room for maneuver.
