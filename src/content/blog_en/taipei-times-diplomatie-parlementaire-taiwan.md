I'm publishing a new op-ed in the **[Taipei Times](https://www.taipeitimes.com)**, Taiwan's leading English-language daily newspaper, founded in 1999. A reference publication on Taiwanese news and international relations in the strait, it regularly hosts analyses from experts and academics from around the world.

## The topic: parliamentary diplomacy as Taiwan's plan B

The article analyzes Taiwan's strategic pivot toward parliamentary diplomacy. After the cancellation of President Lai Ching-te's state visit, caused by Chinese pressure on overflight rights, Taiwan faces a reality: classic diplomatic access at the executive level is increasingly locked down by Beijing.

In response, the island is relying on an alternative channel: parliamentarians. More than 300 European parliamentarians are now involved through friendship groups and caucuses dedicated to Taiwan in numerous parliaments. These legislative delegations have produced concrete results — resolutions on Chinese coercive measures, semiconductor supply chains, Taiwan's participation in international organizations.

The article highlights a paradox: elected officials who freely visit Taiwan lose this capacity as soon as they assume executive functions. It describes a two-track system where executives maintain formal diplomatic ambiguity while legislatures carry out the substantive work that Beijing struggles to block.

I argue for Taiwan to deepen this parliamentary channel by reversing information flows, co-drafting legislation with international parliamentarians, and creating permanent bilateral legislative committees.
