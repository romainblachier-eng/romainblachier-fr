I have just published an article in **Revue Conflits**, titled *"Asian Communists: is the East still red?"*. Revue Conflits is a leading French magazine on geopolitics, international relations and strategic analysis.

## The thesis

Three regimes in Asia continue to claim to be communist — Vietnam, Laos, North Korea — but they have so little in common, ideologically and economically, that the label loses all analytical value. By grouping everything under the same red flag, we prevent ourselves from understanding what is really at stake in each of these countries.

- **Vietnam**: a developmentalist state that pursues growth, courts foreign investors and has seen its per capita income rise from $96 in 1989 to nearly $5,000 in 2025. Hanoi borrows from Park Chung-hee and Lee Kuan Yew, not from Marx.
- **Laos**: follows in Vietnam's footsteps, under heavy Chinese financial dependence, with public debt brought down to 82% of GDP at the end of 2025.
- **North Korea**: the *songbun*, hereditary caste system, and the emergence of the *donju* — the merchant class that prospers in the shadow of the regime — clearly indicate what we are dealing with: extreme right-wing racial nationalism, not a variant of Marxism-Leninism.

## Why it matters

Continuing to speak of "communism" for these three countries means masking the ethno-nationalist nature of Pyongyang's regime and obscuring our understanding of Hanoi and Vientiane's trajectories. Without an accurate diagnosis, there can be no lucid foreign policy — whether regarding economic cooperation, Indo-Pacific strategy or human rights.
