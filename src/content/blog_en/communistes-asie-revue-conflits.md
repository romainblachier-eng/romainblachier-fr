I have just published an article in **Revue Conflits**, titled *"Asian Communists: is the East still red?"*. Revue Conflits is a leading French magazine on geopolitics, international relations and strategic analysis.

## The thesis

Three regimes in Asia continue to claim communism — Vietnam, Laos, North Korea — but they have so little in common, ideologically and economically, that the label loses all analytical value. By grouping everything under the same red flag, we prevent ourselves from understanding what is really at stake in each of these countries.

- **Vietnam**: a developmentalist state that pursues growth, courts foreign investors and has seen its income per capita rise from $96 in 1989 to nearly $5,000 in 2025. Hanoi borrows from Park Chung-hee and Lee Kuan Yew, not Marx.
- **Laos**: follows in Vietnam's footsteps, under strong Chinese financial dependence, with public debt reduced to 82% of GDP at the end of 2025.
- **North Korea**: the *songbun*, hereditary caste system, and the emergence of the *donju* — the merchant class that prospers in the shadow of the regime — clearly show what we are dealing with: far-right racial nationalism, not a variant of Marxism-Leninism.

## Why it matters

Continuing to speak of "communism" for these three countries means masking the ethno-nationalist nature of the Pyongyang regime and blurring the reading of Hanoi and Vientiane's trajectories. Without an accurate diagnosis, there can be no lucid foreign policy — whether it concerns economic cooperation, Indo-Pacific strategy or human rights.
